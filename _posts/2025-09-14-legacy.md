## Exploring the Digital Legacy of Analog-Era Artists: A Network Analysis of Manos Hadjidakis’s Position on Spotify 

![Co-occurrence Network of Manos Hadjidakis](/assets/images/network.png "Artist Network Graph showing Hadjidakis's connections with other Greek musicians") 

Digitization has reshaped how cultural heritage is preserved and accessed, raising concerns
about ownership, accessibility, and the balance between born-digital and analog artifacts. Even though digital
media per se may not be exclusively responsible for “the end of all memory” (Zierold 2010) it is perhaps meaningful to reflect on who has the power to shape the politics of it. This project explored the representation of analog-era artists on digital platforms, focusing on Greek composer Manos Hadjidakis within Spotify’s ecosystem. Using data retrieved through Spotify's API, his presence in playlists, connections with related artists, and his role within a co-occurrence network were analyzed. 

### Methodology

Data on Manos Hadjidakis and related Greek artists (in terms of genre, and career timeframe) were collected from Spotify using a combination of API searches and manually compiled artist keywords from the “Fans Also Like” section. Public playlists containing these artists were retrieved, cleaned, and organized, removing duplicates and irrelevant entries. Statistical analysis measured playlist appearances and co-occurrences, while a co-occurrence network was built with artists as nodes connected if they appeared together in playlists. Degree and betweenness centrality metrics were calculated to assess Hadjidakis’s influence and bridging role within the network. 

---

### Key Takeaways

- **Platform Power in Cultural Memory:** Spotify’s algorithms and curation practices actively shape which artists remain visible, highlighting structural biases in digital cultural preservation.  

- **Value of Network Analysis:** Playlist co-occurrence networks provide a powerful way to quantify visibility, revealing both central figures and marginal actors in cultural ecosystems.  

- **Hadjidakis’s Position:** Despite his cultural significance, Hadjidakis appears in only 8.11% of playlists, showing that analog-era legacies may be underrepresented in digital contexts.  

- **Clustered but Not Central:** His strong ties with other Greek artists suggest recognition within a niche cultural cluster, but low betweenness centrality indicates limited reach beyond that community.  

- **Algorithmic Inequalities:** The “winner-take-all” (Poell, Nieborg, and Duffy 2021) dynamics of streaming platforms favor highly popular or active artists, risking the marginalization of historically important analog figures.  

- **Transparency as a Preservation Imperative:** Without clearer insight into how playlists are generated and promoted, it is difficult to ensure equitable cultural representation.  

- **Future Research Pathways:** Larger datasets, cross-comparisons between user-generated and AI-generated playlists, and analysis of playlist popularity metrics could deepen understanding of how digital platforms preserve—or distort—cultural heritage.
